Three years ago today, the noise stopped.

You know the noise. The idiocracy of corporatocracy, in full voice. Synergy stand-ups. Transformation roadmaps with no roads on them. A “cash flow imperative,” announced from a stage to a room that applauded on cue because the alternative was being noticed. Grown adults crying — about budgets, about headcount, about a dashboard that turned the wrong shade of red — every tear routed through a committee and logged as an action item. Initiatives in the costume of progress. Programs that fed a few while the rest performed gratitude. Every simple fix buried under a hurdle built by whoever could shout the loudest with the least to say.

I’d found the design — the system that would have saved them millions a year. The actual cash flow imperative, sitting in a drawer nobody opened. I brought it to the table. There was no table. There was a meeting I wasn’t invited to, and a decision made before anyone learned my name.

So the verdict came down. The adults stopped crying. Or I stopped having to watch.

And a baby started.

Different crying. The real kind. The miracle-of-life kind, where a brand-new person announces at full volume that they exist, they’re hungry, and you’re the one. No approval chain. No return date. No portal asking me to rate my experience. Best paternity leave in corporate history, approved by nobody.

Then the hobbies got out of hand.

**[fredgar-ai](https://github.com/bamr87/fredgar-ai)** reads SEC filings and FRED data and writes the summary. A machine that actually reads cash flow statements — which puts it ahead of the imperative.

**[law-ai](https://github.com/bamr87/law-ai)** keeps a lawyer on retainer who never sleeps. Every pull request gets cross-examined. The ones carrying license or compliance risk never leave the building.

**[zer0-CMS](https://github.com/bamr87/zer0-CMS)**, cut loose from a Front Matter fork and rebuilt on Django and Wagtail. SQLite or Postgres. Ships in a container. No Ruby left in the body.

**[lifehacker.dev](https://lifehacker.dev/)** — drafted by a robot, reviewed by a human, published with the mistakes left in. The mistakes are the evidence.

**[csv-vscoode](https://github.com/bash-365/csv-vscoode)** puts the spreadsheet inside the editor, so you never leave the scene to fix a column.

And the thing they wouldn’t let me build: a control plane over forty repos. A registry that rewrites the README every night and fails any pull request that lies about it. Eyes on every service. Infrastructure reconciled from git. Agents that triage, implement, review, release. A phone in my pocket that watches the whole fleet. Nothing in that building has to align. It just has to pass CI.

[github.com/bamr87/bamr87](https://github.com/bamr87/bamr87)

Three weeks ago, the crying came back. Three in the morning, every night. No agenda, no slide deck, no action items. Just a small person who means every word. First meeting I’ve been glad to attend in years.

They had a cash flow imperative. I built one that works, and I left the door unlocked.

Same table. Better seat.
